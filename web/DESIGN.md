# ocra Cloud console: design spec

The rules every page of the web console follows. Values live in `web/src/styles/base.css`. This file says how to use them and why. A change to a rule edits this file in the same pull request. Rules marked **(test)** fail `npm test` when broken.

## Principles

- **A quiet frame for loud data.** Chrome is neutral and recedes; colour is spent on what the review found, never on navigation or decoration.
- **One ink for actions.** Primary actions are ink on panel; there is no brand accent colour.
- **Red is a warning, not a style.** Red marks only critical findings, errors and what cannot be undone, never links, tabs or selected states. The one exception is a diff, where removed lines and counts take a red tint and added ones a green tint.
- **Dense, not cramped.** 13 px body text, 8 px spacing steps, hairlines instead of boxes.
- **Every state is designed:** loading (skeleton), empty, error, and phone width, in light and dark.
- **Theme:** the system's by default; the account menu and the command menu choose light, dark or the system's, kept in this browser (`web/src/theme.ts`). `public/theme.js` sets `data-theme` on `<html>` before the first paint, and dark tokens hang off `:root[data-theme="dark"]`, never `prefers-color-scheme`.

## Tokens

Pages and components style only through the custom properties in `base.css`.

- **Colour (test):** no colour literal (`#…`, `rgb()`, `hsl()`, `oklch()`) in any stylesheet but `base.css`. Mix tokens with `color-mix()` where a tint is needed. No component or inline style names one either: no hex or `rgb()` value and no Tailwind palette class in a `.ts` or `.tsx` file **(test)**.
- **Text tones:** `--t1` body and headings, `--t2` secondary text, `--t3` labels and metadata, `--t4` placeholders and disabled text only. `--t1` to `--t3` keep 4.5:1 contrast on the panel and the ground, in both themes **(test, `contrast.test.ts`)**.
- **Surfaces:** `--ground` behind everything, `--panel` for the page, `--raised` for cards and menus, `--field` for inputs and selects. Depth has three levels and no others: flat on the panel with a hairline, `--shadow` (resting: cards), `--float` (above the page: menus, drawers, dialogs), never from borders. A field's edge is `--field-edge`: the resting shadow in light; in dark, where a field would vanish into the card it sits on, a surface one step lighter with an outline button's edge.
- **Lines:** `--hair` between rows, `--hair2` under headers and around groups. Lines are 0.5 px.
- **Fills:** `--fill` at rest and under a hovered row (a list row, a tree link, a settings link); `--fill2` for the selected row and for a hovered control (an icon button, a chip, a tile, a menu option). A fold's row has no fill of its own: its chevron darkens to `--t1` under the pointer. A `<summary>` that folds carries `.fold` and never the browser's marker.
- **Status tones:** `--critical` for critical findings, errors and destructive actions, `--warning` for warnings; pages name these semantic tokens, not `--red` and `--amber`, which are the palette behind them. `--green` is ok, `--blue` running or informational. Use them on text and soft fills (badges, notes), never as large backgrounds. Each reads at 4.5:1 as text on its own badge fill (an 8% tint), on the panel, on a card and inside a selected row, in both themes **(test, `contrast.test.ts`)**.
- **Chart series:** `--c1` to `--c6`, in order of use.
- **Radii (test, `styles.test.ts`):** `--r-tag` 4, `--r-chip` and `--r-tab` 6, `--r-ctl` 8 (controls, cards), `--r-sheet` 10 (panel, drawer). No stylesheet writes another corner; round ends (`99px` on a meter's track, `50%` on a dot) and a hairline's 1 or 2 px are shapes, not steps.
- **coss ui names** (`--background`, `--popover`, `--accent`, `--destructive`, …) are aliases of the same tokens, declared in `base.css`; `styles/coss.css` maps them into Tailwind.

## Type

- **Fonts:** Geist for text, JetBrains Mono for code, diffs, file paths and ids, Noto Sans SC for Chinese (both stacks fall through to it). Ligatures are off everywhere: code reads as typed.
- **Scale (test):** sizes only through `--text-2xs` (10) … `--text-4xl` (30); no literal font sizes in stylesheets. Body is `--text-base` (13).
- **Roles:** page title `--text-2xl`/600; section and card titles `--text-base`/600; table headers, stat labels and group labels (`.quiet-label`) `--text-sm`/500 in sentence case, `--t3`, never uppercase or tracked; big numbers `--text-3xl`/600 with tabular figures.
- **Line heights:** 20 px for body text, 16 px for labels, tags and code chips at `--text-sm` and under, 28 px for a page title and a big number. A `font` shorthand is followed by its `line-height`, since the shorthand resets it.
- **Weights:** 400, 500 and 600; `b` and `strong` are 600. A card's title is an `h2` (`.card-head h2` or `.card-title`), never a `div` at 500.
- **Numbers** in tables, stats and badges use tabular figures and align right in columns (`.num`).
- **Times of day** go through `clock` (`web/src/format.ts`): the viewer's clock, the hour without a leading zero ("4:00 PM").
- Chinese text gets no letter-spacing.

## Spacing and layout

- **Steps (test, `styles.test.ts`):** 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 px, with 2 and 6 for the gap between an icon or mark and its label. Every padding, gap and margin in a stylesheet is one of them or a token; a margin under 8 px may be any value, since it is an optical nudge (a dot centred on a line of text), not a step of the layout. An offset that follows from other sizes is written as the sum (`calc()`), not as its result.
- **Keyline:** everything in a card starts and ends `--gutter` (16 px) from the card's edge: its title, its padding, a toolbar, a table row's first and last cell, a list row, a settings row, a stat, a code block. A checkbox sits on the keyline too (no browser margin). An icon button may reach past it so its glyph, not its hover box, ends on the line.
- **Rhythm:** the page's content starts 24 px under the page bar and 24 px under the page head; sections are 32 px apart (`.section`); the parts of one section (an add form and its list, stats and their charts, a card and its footnote) are 12 px apart. Table cells are 8×12, so a one-line row is 36 px, the same as a list row.
- **Control heights (test, `styles.test.ts`):** two, from `--ctl-h` (28 px: buttons at `size="sm"`, inputs, selects, segmented controls, icon buttons, tabs) and `--ctl-h-lg` (32 px: a page's own actions in its head, and a view's one primary action). Controls on one line share one height: a line of fields ends in a `size="sm"` button. Both grow by 4 px under 640 px, where the coss ui controls grow for touch. No stylesheet sets a control's height in pixels. A control's text sits `--ctl-pad` (10 px) from its edge, and controls take `--r-ctl`.
- **Frame:** a 56 px icon rail on the ground, the page on one panel beside it (`.frame`, `.rail`, `.panel`). The pagebar on top of the panel shows the breadcrumb and the place's tabs.
- **Places:** Inbox, Reviews, Findings, Repositories, Insights, and Settings as its own mode with a grouped side list. A new page belongs to one place and appears as one of its tabs (`routes.tsx`); it is reachable from the command menu (Ctrl/⌘ K) automatically, with its place's icon; every page is under one Go to group, settings pages under their group, then Theme, Help and Account; before anything is typed it leads with the last five pull requests opened in this browser (`recent-pulls.ts`, forgotten with the cache on sign-out). The same menu searches: past two characters it lists what the server finds (pull requests, reviews, findings, repositories) under the matching pages, a pull request named by its reference first.
- **Content width:** 1120 px max, centred, for lists, settings and reports. Work pages (below) take the panel's full width.
- **Breakpoints:** 1100 px (a work page's side columns stack), 900 px (grids and stat rows reflow), 760 px (the frame: rail becomes a row), 560 px. A new media query uses one of these.
- **Phone (760 px and narrower):** the rail becomes a row across the top, the panel fills the width, settings become a scrolling row, and wide tables stack. Nothing scrolls the page sideways at 390 px.

## Page patterns

- **Page head:** on a place's own tab the bar over the page already names it, so the head keeps only its actions on one line, right-aligned, and the title is for screen readers (`NamedByBar`); the sentence on what the page is for goes, but a `note` saying what the page does with the user's code or data stays as a quiet line. Settings pages, pages under a place (a review, a live run) and an error keep the title and one sentence, actions on the right on the title's line. A filter of the whole page (a range, a kind) is one of its actions.
- **Cards** (`.card`, `.card-head`, `.card-pad`) group one subject. Don't nest cards.
- **Tables** for records the user compares; list rows for records the user opens. A row of one text line is one height whatever it holds: a badge or a platform mark does not make it taller. Row actions sit at the row's right end, quietest first, destructive last. A table that would outgrow its card with them puts them behind one menu button (`Ellipsis`, named "Actions for …"). A table goes in `StackTable`: when its card is too narrow to hold it, at any window width, each row becomes a block (first cell as its title, the rest flowing after it, a `.cell-name` caption where a column head carried the meaning) instead of scrolling inside the card.
- **Stats row:** up to four `.stat` cards, label above value, a note below.
- **Detail pages:** properties in a left column, the timeline of what happened on the right.
- **Work pages** (a pull request, a walkthrough): a sticky side column of what the user navigates the work with (its files), the work itself (the diff) in the main column, and an optional third column (the impact rail) that stacks under the main one at 1100 px. A pull request's files are a tree (`FileList`, `web/src/styles/pull-files.css`): flat on the panel, a folder row over its files with a chain of single folders joined into one, each row with a 16 px icon of its kind (`FileTypeIcon`), a hairline down each level it sits under, and after a file's name a tick once viewed, a letter for what happened to it (A added, D removed, R renamed) and its line counts; past eight files a field narrows it by path. The diffs follow the tree's order (`treeOrder`). A file's head (its path on one line, the folders giving way to an ellipsis before its name, its counts, Viewed) stays in view under the page's head while its diff scrolls by. A diff is set at `--text-base` on a 22 px line. Its code is coloured by language (`highlightDiff`, `web/src/pull/highlight.ts`): the highlighter and each grammar load when a diff first needs them, a token's kind takes one of the console's hues mixed toward the text colour (`web/src/styles/syntax.css`) so it reads on added and removed lines in both themes, and a file of an unknown kind, of more than 4,000 lines or with a line past 1,000 characters stays plain. A diff is unified or split (`DiffBar`, the choice kept in this browser; a phone is always unified); split sets a removed line beside the line that replaced it, a side with no line left blank on `--fill`. Long lines wrap, hanging under their text; Wrap lines in the same bar (kept in this browser; a phone always wraps) turns that off, and then a file's diff scrolls sideways in its own box, every run as wide as the file's longest line (`--line-ch`), its hunk heads and notes held in view and, unified, its line numbers held at the left edge behind a hairline. Within a changed line the words that changed carry a stronger tint of the line's own colour (`wordChanges`, `web/src/pull/diff-words.ts`); a line rewritten past 60 % is left whole. Lock files, generated code and vendored copies start folded with a badge naming which (`foldReason`, `web/src/pull/generated.ts`). A diff is drawn in runs of 48 rows, each only while it is within 1,600 px of the window (`LazyBody`), and coloured a few hunks at a time once its file comes near, so a pull request of thousands of lines scrolls like a short one; a run holding a comment being written stays drawn. A Markdown file (`.md`, `.markdown`) not removed has Diff and Rendered beside Viewed (`RenderedMarkdown`, `web/src/pull/markdown-view.tsx`): Rendered reads the whole file at the head shown through the same lines route, up to 5,000 lines, and draws it through the description's allowlist as the description's sheet type, no wider than 76 ch; the diff stays the default, and a find that steps into the file shows the diff again. A changed file's hunk heads carry the lines its diff left out above them (`useContextLines`, read at the head shown through `GET /api/pulls/lines`): arrow buttons open 20 at a time from the change above or below, one button opens a gap of 20 or fewer, and a quiet count says how many are still hidden; the last hunk is followed by a row that opens down to the file's end, offered only once the lines after it have been read ahead (when the diff first comes near the window) and there are some. ⌘F or Ctrl+F on a pull request opens a find over its diffs (`useDiffSearch`, `SearchBar`): it searches every line of every file, drawn or not, folded or not, says "3 of 41", steps with Enter and Shift+Enter, opens the file of the match and scrolls it to the middle; matches take a blue tint over any changed-word tint and the current one a stronger tint with a 1 px ring. The same keys from its field give the browser's own find back, and Escape closes it. While it is open its bar stays in view where a file's head would, and the file heads stick under it. In the rail, a line said of the row over it (a symbol with no uses) has no top padding: it sits with that row, closer than the next row does.
- **A pull request's page** (`web/src/styles/pull.css`) is wider than other pages (1360 px) and adds two things above its code. Its head: where it lives (repository and number, a link to the platform) on a quiet line, then the title at `--text-3xl` beside the actions at `--ctl-h-lg`, of which the next step is the one primary button (mark ready on a draft, merge once nothing is in the way, review otherwise), and one meta line (`PullMeta`): first the standing as `Status`, an icon and a word in its colour ( draft, conflicts, ready; while it is in a merge queue, the queue's word for it, the same one its card and its inbox row show), never a chip in the row of buttons, then the author with their mark, the branch into its base as two chips, files and lines, when it last moved. The head scrolls away with the page; once it is gone, one 48 px line takes its place under the page bar (`CompactBar`): the number in quiet text, the title on one line, the standing and the same actions, so the diff gets the window and the next step stays in reach. It takes no room in the page, enters with a 4 px rise at `--d-move`, and is not drawn under 760 px, where the page bar wraps. Under it, what the pull request is (stack: a line per layer, bottom first, joined down their check marks, a fork's further branches a step to the right, each with what stands out about it at its right end (needs restack in the warning colour, the one note that asks for something; draft and how its review stands in quiet text), then what this layer lacks of the one under it beside the button that brings it in, where the last merge stopped and why, and the merge; or its merge while one is under way: a line per layer with its mark, what the current one waits for, and Stop; `d` and `u` move down and up the stack; description, threads) sits beside a status rail of what it waits on, ordered by what blocks first: merge readiness with unmet requirements leading and met ones folded (it counts what is met and leaves the standing to the head's `Status`), and Update branch under them while the branch is behind its base and an update can fix it, merge (on a branch with a merge queue, the queue instead: its state in a badge, what that state means in a sentence, its place, when it should merge and since when on one quiet line, the pull requests ahead of it, and Remove; out of the queue, how it last left (the platform's reason, or who took it out) over Add, which is the primary button once queueing is the next step, with one quiet line while a requirement is unmet saying the queue takes it once Merge readiness is met; the card reads the queue again every 10 s while the pull request is in it), ocra's review, checks, reviewers, labels; a section with nothing in it says so on its title's line ("No one asked", "None yet") rather than under it, and ocra's two writes sit side by side, so the rail stays short beside a short description. Nothing in this overview is a card but two things read as documents: its sections are flat, set apart by space on the left and by hairlines in the rail; the description is a sheet (`--text-md`, headings at `--text-xl` and `--text-lg`), and each review thread is a card: where it is on a quiet bar (file icon, path and line, standing, Resolve), its comments under it with the author's initial beside each, the reply at the foot. A review thread's comments are drawn as Markdown (`ThreadBody`), HTML comments and suggestion blocks out of sight; ocra's comment of a finding leads with its title, a severity badge and its notes on a quiet line, and folds the evidence it ends with under `Evidence`. A finding's own text and suggested fix are that Markdown on every page that shows them (`FindingText`), and the review page folds its evidence under the same `Evidence` disclosure. On the diff, a finding ends with Mark fixed and Dismiss (`FindingDecide`): it leaves the diff at once with Undo in a toast, and a dismissal asks its reason, and whether to remember it, on the same line. A list of requirements carries a `Tally` over it. At 1100 px the page is one column: the stack, then the rail, then the read. A "Jump to code" button floats while the diff is below the window. While its files are read the page holds its own frame (`PullLoading`): the repository line, a title and a meta line of their real height, the overview beside the rail; a pull request the platform does not find says so with Back to inbox (`PullFailed`).
- **Creating and editing:** a one-line create (a name and a value) sits inline in an `.add-form` card above the list; anything with more fields opens in a drawer, so the list stays in view. In a drawer every label, legend, field and choice starts on the drawer's 24 px edge, a field's label is medium weight like a group's legend, and a choice's box sits on the middle of its text.
- **Actions and standings:** a line that holds buttons holds only buttons and text. How something stands (draft, conflicts, resolved, armed, committed) is never shaped like a control there: it is `Status`, a mark and a word, set before the actions (on a card's title line, after the title). Buttons are flat (one fill or one hairline edge, no shadow, no inset highlight **(test)**), and the buttons of one line share one size. The one primary (`default`) is the next step; the rest are `outline`. A filled `.badge` belongs in a row of a table or list, never inside a button **(test)** nor beside a line's actions.
- **Confirming:** no dialogs. An action that cannot be undone (merge, delete data, revoke a key) confirms in place: its button becomes "Confirm: …" beside Cancel, in the same spot. Removing something the user can add back again needs no confirmation.
- **Errors:** a failed load shows `LoadError` with a retry where the content would be, in the same frame (`inCard` inside a card; cards never nest); a failed action shows `ActionError` above the list it acted on. A page never shows an empty state for data that has not loaded or failed to load.
- **Empty states** say what will appear here and the one action that makes it appear. A whole view gets `EmptyState`; a part of a card (one chart, a sub-list) gets `EmptyNote`, one quiet centred line.
- **First run:** until an account's pull requests get reviewed, the inbox and Usage lead with `GetStarted` (`web/src/first-run/`): the steps in order, ticked from what the console already reads, and only the step to do now opens, with why it matters and its one button; the inbox lets the person hide it. A page that would take a step the account cannot finish yet says so where the step starts (Auto review without a model key: `NoKeyNotice`), with a link to the missing step.
- **Loading:** `Loading` draws list rows (a title and a short meta, 40 px each) in the frame the content will fill, so the page head, cards and error share one place; its bars fade in after 300 ms, so fast loads never flash; cached data shows at once and refreshes in place. Settings pages wait with `PrefsPending`.
- **Getting ahead of the click:** a link's page loads its code, and its first read, while the pointer rests on it, the keyboard selects it, or it is focused; a press or a navigation starts every read the page lists (`reads` in `routes.tsx`). The likely pages' code loads while the browser is idle. A switch or choice shows its new value at once and goes back if the save is refused.

## Components

Shared controls are coss ui (Base UI + Tailwind), copied into `web/src/components/ui/` with imports rewritten to `@/components/ui` and `@/lib/utils`. Tailwind utilities work only inside `web/src/components`; pages style with their own CSS (`web/src/styles/*.css`, no cascade layers).

| Need | Use |
|---|---|
| Action | `Button`: `default` primary (one per view), `outline` everything else, `ghost` in dense rows, `destructive-outline` to remove, `destructive` only inside a confirmation |
| Link that looks like a button | `<Button render={<Link to="…" />}>` |
| On/off setting | `Switch` |
| One of a few options, all visible | `Seg` (coss Toggle Group) |
| One of many options | native `select.input` |
| Several independent options in a form | `.check` checkboxes |
| A status word in a table or list row | `.badge` (`-ok`, `-warn`, `-run`, `-err`): a 20 px tag with `--r-tag` corners, smaller and squarer than any control |
| A standing beside actions, in a head or on a card's title line | `Status` (`web/src/components/status.tsx`): a 14 px mark and the word in its tone, no fill and no edge |
| A sentence of status or help | `.note` (`.ok`, `.err`) |
| An empty list, a failed load, a missing page | `EmptyState` (coss Empty): icon, what appears here, one action |
| An empty part of a card | `EmptyNote` (`.empty`): one centred `--t3` line |
| A new account's setup | `GetStarted`: an ordered list with a meter, done steps ticked, the current step's reason and one button |
| Data on its way | `Loading` (`.skeleton`): row bars in the content's frame, `inCard` inside a card |
| Text entry | `input.input`, `textarea.input` |
| Name of an icon-only control | `Tooltip` (the rail's `RailTip`); `aria-label` always |
| A few actions behind a button | `Menu` |
| A short note after something happened | `useToast()(message, tone, action?)` (coss Toast), `"success"` or `"error"` when it is one; never a line in the page that pushes content down. A step that can be taken back offers `{ label: "Undo", run }`, and the toast stays 6 s |
| A write the platform will almost surely take (resolve, reopen, reply) | shown at once, before the answer; taken back with an error toast if refused (`web/src/pull/threads.tsx`) |
| Go anywhere | the command menu (`Command`) |
| A finding's checked fix | `FixProposal` on a diff, `FixDiff` in a finding (`web/src/fixes/`): the lines it replaces, then what it writes, and Commit fix |
| ocra's open finding on a pull request's diff | `FindingNote` (`web/src/pull/findings.tsx`): under the last line it is about, above that line's fix, with its severity, title, text and a link into the review; only for the review of the head shown |
| A pull request's reviewers and assignees | `PeopleCard` (`web/src/pull/people.tsx`): each login with where they stand, a cross to take off one still asked or assigned, and Add, which opens `PeoplePicker` in place: a field that lists the repository's people by login prefix; Escape closes it |
| Text a model wrote | `Markdown` (`web/src/markdown.tsx`, `styles/markdown.css`): headings at their level (from `h3`), lists, tables, code and emphasis; links, images and HTML stay text, since a model's answer is about untrusted code |
| Text a person wrote on the platform (a description, a review comment) | `PlatformMarkdown` (`web/src/platform-markdown.tsx`): GitHub-flavored Markdown with the HTML the platform allows (folds, tables, task lists, `kbd`, `sub`/`sup`), from an allowlist of tags and attributes, no comments; `https` links open in a new tab, any other address is dropped, and a picture becomes a link to it (the Content-Security-Policy loads none from another host) |
| A person on a line | `PersonMark` (`web/src/person-mark.tsx`): their picture where the server passed one the content policy allows, their initial on `--fill2` otherwise; the login follows it |
| How a pull request's checks stand, on a row | `CheckMark` (`web/src/pull/check-mark.tsx`): a 14 px circle icon in the state's tone (check, cross, dashed while running), named for a screen reader; an empty box of the same width where unknown, so titles stay aligned |
| A list's keys | `KeyHints` (`web/src/inbox/key-hints.tsx`): key caps (`Kbd`) with what each does, once under the list at `--text-sm`; never in the page's opening line |
| How a list of requirements stands | `Tally` (`web/src/pull/tally.tsx`): a 3 px line over the list, a segment per state as wide as its share (green met, red blocking, amber warning, `--t4` waiting), named for a screen reader |

A new shared control comes from coss ui first, then from a small component in `web/src/components/` styled with tokens.

## Icons

- **Lucide** for every UI icon, imported per icon (`lucide-react/icons/<name>`) so the bundle carries only what it uses **(test)**. Navigation icons are 20 px, inline icons 16 px, a mark beside small text 14 px and a mark in a chip or a disc 12 px; no other size **(test)**. One weight: the stylesheet draws every Lucide icon at 1.75 stroke (`base.css`), the open page's navigation icon at 2 and a 12 px mark at 2.25 so its line matches a 16 px icon's; a component never sets `strokeWidth` **(test)**.
- Product marks are the products' own, drawn in the text colour so they read the same in both themes: GitHub and GitLab through `PlatformMark` (simple-icons), model providers through `ProviderLogo`, a quiet tile holding the mark from simple-icons or, where it has none, from `@lobehub/icons-static-svg` (`provider-marks.ts`). Every provider the gateway lists has one (the type fails otherwise); only a name it does not list falls back to two letters.
- **Files and folders** are the one exception to Lucide: a file tree, a diff's head and a thread's head show the kind of file with Material Icon Theme's icon (`FileTypeIcon`, `web/src/pull/file-icon.tsx`; MIT), in its own colours, 16 px. Only the kinds listed there are bundled; any other gets the plain file or folder.
- An icon never carries meaning alone: it has a visible label or an accessible name and tooltip.

## Motion

- **Easings:** `--e-std` for state changes, `--e-enter` for things that appear, `--e-settle` for things that move into place.
- **Durations (test, `styles.test.ts`):** four tokens, `--d-press` (80 ms, press feedback), `--d-fast` (120 ms, what changes colour: hover, selection) and `--d-move` (160 ms, what changes place or size: a fold's chevron, the drawer, a menu) and `--d-settle` (220 ms, on `--e-settle`, what travels: the gliding highlight, a fold opening or closing). No stylesheet writes a duration in a transition or animation. Nothing longer, except the skeleton's fade-in and its shimmer, work under way (`.shimmer-text` on its label with the seconds beside it, a task's ring), and the coss ui components' own tuned motion (a toast's stack settles over half a second), which pages leave as it is.
- **Hover and press:** every hover and press a page draws itself takes one rule (`web/src/styles/motion.css`): colours fade over 120 ms, and an icon button or tile gives way (scale 0.96) over 80 ms on press. A new hoverable class joins that list rather than setting its own transition.
- **Gliding highlight:** a list of links or choices draws one highlight for the whole list (`.glide`, `web/src/glide.ts`) that moves to the item under the pointer or to the picked one, rather than a fill on each item: the rail, the settings links, a place's tabs, the segmented control, a menu's items (where it follows the keys too). It appears in place and only glides between items.
- **Copying:** a copy button (`CopyButton`, `web/src/copy-button.tsx`) answers in place: its icon turns into a check for a moment and a screen reader hears what was copied. No toast.
- **What moves:** hover and selected states fade; menus, tooltips and toasts scale or rise in from their anchor; drawers slide from the right; nothing animates on scroll and nothing loops but loading.
- **Continuity:** an element that stays on screen keeps its position across a state change (optimistic updates, no reflow flashes); the open place and tab stay highlighted.
- **Reduced motion:** under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every transition and animation stops and shows its end state.

## Accessibility

- Focus is always visible, drawn only in `--focus` **(test, `styles.test.ts`)**, the solid `--blue`, which keeps 3:1 against every surface **(test, `contrast.test.ts`)**. The ring sits 2 px outside a control; a row, tab or tree link that reaches the edge of its card or scrolling strip draws it inside (`base.css`), because the card clips what is outside.
- A switch that is off is still seen: its track is `--switch-off`, which keeps 3:1 against the panel and a card in both themes, as its thumb does against the track **(test, `contrast.test.ts`)**.
- **The page bar's crumb:** a page under a place (a pull request, a review) names itself after the place, and the place is a link back. A pull request opened from the inbox also shows where it stands there ("2 of 8") with previous and next, `[` and `]` (`web/src/inbox/queue-nav.tsx`); the inbox's order is kept only while the console is open.
- **A page's commands** (`usePageCommands`, `web/src/page-commands.ts`): what a work page does is said once, as a label, an action and at most one unmodified key. The command menu lists them first under the page's name with the key beside each, the shortcuts panel lists them, and the key runs the same action on the page, never while typing, in a drawer or a menu. A card may offer its own under the page's title (the ocra review card's `a`). Keys keep one meaning everywhere: `j`/`k` next and previous, `Enter` open here, `o` open on the platform, `x` select, `u` read or unread, `/` find, `r` review or refresh, `a` ask ocra to review, `v` viewed, `d` details or dismiss, `f` fixed, `c` code, `m` merge, `n`/`N` next and previous finding, `[`/`]` previous and next pull request.
- **A list read by keys** (`useListCursor`, `web/src/list-cursor.ts`): `j`/`k` move a current row, kept in view and tinted `.is-current`. The inbox always shows it; elsewhere it shows from the first key press, at the first row.
- **A list that refreshes under the pointer** (the inbox, `useHeldOrder`): while the pointer is over it, rows keep their places and only their contents change; the new order lands when the pointer leaves, so the row about to be clicked never slides away.
- **The inbox's columns** (`ColumnHead`, `web/src/inbox/row.tsx`): one quiet 32 px line at `--text-xs` over the first section names them (Pull request, Repository, Status, Size, Author, Updated) in the rows' own column classes, so it lines up with them at every width; a column a row drops drops its name too, and under 900 px, where the columns give way, the line goes.
- **Keys everywhere** (`web/src/shortcuts/`): `?` opens the shortcuts panel (also in the command menu under Help), `g` then a place's letter goes there (`g i` inbox, `g r` reviews, `g f` findings, `g p` repositories, `g n` insights, `g s` settings; the command menu shows them beside each place, and so does each place's tip on the rail), ⌘K (Ctrl+K) the command menu. Keys are written with `KeyCaps`.
- **Drawers hold focus:** a drawer takes focus to its first field (else the panel) when it opens, keeps Tab inside it, closes on Escape, and gives focus back to what had it.
- Every control is reachable and usable by keyboard; menus and the command menu follow the ARIA menu and combobox patterns (from Base UI).
- Touch targets are at least 32 px; tone contrast as above.
